If you've started researching rooftop solar, you've hit three words that all sound similar: on-grid, off-grid and hybrid. They describe how your system relates to the grid and to batteries — and the right choice changes both your cost and what happens during a power cut. Here's a plain comparison, with no invented prices, to help you shortlist before a survey.

## Quick overview of the three types

- **On-grid** — connected to the grid, no batteries. Cheapest, uses net metering, but no backup during outages.
- **Off-grid** — batteries, no reliance on the grid. Full independence, highest cost.
- **Hybrid** — grid *and* batteries. Backup during cuts plus net-metering export. Higher cost than on-grid.

## On-grid: lowest cost, net metering, but no backup

An on-grid system is the most popular choice for homes with a reliable grid supply. It's the lowest-cost option because there are no batteries, and it works with [net metering](/blog/net-metering-tangedco-guide) so surplus daytime generation earns you credits. The catch: for safety, on-grid systems shut off during a grid outage, so they don't power your home during a power cut.

**Best for:** homes with a dependable grid supply that want the fastest payback and don't need outage backup.

## Off-grid: full independence with batteries

An off-grid system runs entirely on solar plus battery storage, with no grid connection needed. It gives you complete independence — useful where the grid is absent or extremely unreliable — but batteries add significant cost and need eventual replacement, and the system must be sized carefully to get you through cloudy spells.

**Best for:** remote sites or places where the grid is effectively unavailable.

## Hybrid: grid + battery backup, best of both

A hybrid system connects to the grid *and* includes batteries. During the day it powers your home and can export surplus; during an outage, the battery keeps your essential loads running. You get net-metering benefits and backup — at a higher cost than on-grid because of the battery.

**Best for:** homes that want bill savings *and* protection against frequent power cuts.

## How to choose: a simple decision guide

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **How reliable is my grid?** Reliable → on-grid is often enough. Frequent cuts → consider hybrid.
2. **Do I need backup during outages?** Yes → hybrid (or off-grid). No → on-grid.
3. **What's my budget and payback priority?** Lowest cost and fastest payback → on-grid. Willing to invest for backup/independence → hybrid or off-grid.

There's no single "best" system — only the best fit for your grid, budget and backup needs.
