"How much does solar cost?" is the first question almost every Madurai homeowner asks us — and the honest answer is: it depends. The good news is that the things it depends on are straightforward, and once you understand them you can judge any quote you receive. This guide breaks down what goes into the price, what a system of each size suits, and why the cheapest quote is rarely the best value.

We won't quote you a single fixed rupee figure here, because an accurate price needs a look at your roof and your electricity bill. What we *can* do is explain exactly what moves the number.

## What goes into the cost of a solar system

A rooftop solar system is not just panels. A fair quote covers all of these parts:

- **Solar panels** — the largest single component; tier-1 panels cost more but last and perform better.
- **Inverter** — converts solar DC into usable AC; quality here decides reliability and warranty.
- **Mounting structure** — the frames that hold panels at the right angle, sized for local wind loads.
- **Civil work** — foundations, cable routing and any roof preparation.
- **Wiring, safety and protection** — cabling, earthing, and safety gear that keep the system compliant.
- **Installation labour** — done properly by a trained in-house team, not sub-contracted away.
- **Paperwork** — subsidy application and TANGEDCO net metering, which take time and expertise.

When you compare two quotes, make sure they include the *same* scope. A cheaper number often leaves out civil work, safety gear, or the paperwork you'll then have to chase yourself.

## Typical home system sizes and what they suit

System size is measured in kilowatts (kW). As a rough guide for Madurai homes:

- **1–2 kW** suits a small home with a modest monthly bill.
- **3 kW** is the most common home size — it fits a typical family home and pairs neatly with the subsidy.
- **5 kW** suits larger homes with air-conditioning and higher usage.

The right size depends on your actual consumption, not a rule of thumb. Our [residential rooftop solar](/services/residential-rooftop-solar) page has a sizing guide, but a free survey confirms it.

## What lowers your cost: the PM Surya Ghar subsidy

The single biggest lever on your net price is the central government subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana. Under current government guidelines, a typical 3 kW home system can attract up to ₹78,000 in subsidy, credited to your bank account after installation. Exact amounts and eligibility are set by the government and can change, so treat any figure as indicative until confirmed during your application.

We handle the whole application for you — see our [PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy) for how it works and to estimate your benefit.

## How to think about payback, not just price

Price is what you pay once. Savings are what you collect for the next 25 years. That's why payback matters more than sticker price.

For most Madurai homes, a well-sized system offsets the large majority of the monthly TANGEDCO bill, and typical payback lands in the region of three to five years — after which the power is essentially free for the remaining life of the panels. Your own payback depends on your bill size, the subsidy, and how much sun your roof gets, so treat these as typical ranges, not a promise.

## Why the cheapest quote isn't the best

Solar is a 25-year decision, so the components and the installer matter as much as the price:

- **Component quality** — cheap panels and inverters can underperform and fail early.
- **Warranties** — look for 25-year panel and 10-year inverter warranties, and check they're real manufacturer warranties.
- **After-sales service** — a local team that answers the phone and shows up is worth a great deal over two decades.

A rock-bottom quote that skimps on any of these usually costs more in the long run.
