For many Tamil Nadu farmers, irrigation is one of the biggest and least predictable running costs. Diesel prices climb, and grid power for pumpsets can be limited or arrive at inconvenient hours. Solar water pumps offer a way out — reliable daytime water at close to zero running cost. Here's how farm solar works, what the PM-KUSUM scheme offers, and what to consider before you start.

## The problem: diesel costs and an unreliable grid

Diesel pumps are expensive to run and maintain, and their cost only rises. Grid-powered pumpsets depend on supply that isn't always available when your crops need water. Both leave irrigation at the mercy of factors outside your control — and eat into already thin margins.

## How solar water pumps work on a farm

A solar water pump uses panels to run the pump directly from sunlight. During daylight — exactly when evaporation is highest and crops need water — the panels drive a **surface** or **submersible** pump to draw from your well, borewell or water source. There's no diesel to buy and no dependence on grid timing. Systems can be sized to your water depth and flow needs.

## The benefits

- **Near-zero running cost** — sunshine is free; no diesel bills.
- **Reliable daytime irrigation** — water when the sun (and your crops) need it.
- **Low maintenance** — solar pumps have fewer moving parts than diesel engines.
- **Long life** — quality panels last for decades with basic care.
- **Protection from power-cut losses** — you're not waiting on grid supply.

## The PM-KUSUM scheme in plain terms

PM-KUSUM is a government scheme designed to support farmers in adopting solar — including standalone solar pumps and, in some components, grid-connected agricultural solar. The aim is to reduce farmers' dependence on diesel and grid power and, where applicable, let them benefit from the solar they generate.

Importantly, **eligibility and subsidy levels under PM-KUSUM vary by state, component and year**, and the scheme has different parts with different rules. So treat any specific figure or eligibility claim as something to verify for your situation before relying on it. We help eligible farmers check the current position and complete the paperwork.

## What to consider before you start

A few practical factors decide the right system:

- **Water depth** — how far down your water source sits affects pump type and power.
- **Flow needs** — how much water you need, and how quickly.
- **Acreage and crop** — your irrigation pattern shapes the sizing.
- **Site conditions** — space and sunlight for the panels.

Getting these right is the difference between a pump that comfortably meets your needs and one that struggles.
