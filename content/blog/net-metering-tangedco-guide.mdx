Net metering is the mechanism that turns your rooftop into a two-way relationship with the grid — and it's central to how solar saves you money in Tamil Nadu. This guide explains what it is, how it works day to day with TANGEDCO, and what the paperwork involves. Procedures can change, so treat the process description as a general guide and confirm current TANGEDCO requirements before applying.

## What net metering is

With a normal connection, electricity only flows one way: from the grid to your home. Net metering makes it two-way. When your solar system generates more than you're using, the surplus flows back to the grid and you're **credited** for it. When you need more than you're generating — at night, for instance — you draw from the grid as usual. You're billed on the **net**: what you drew minus what you exported.

## How it works day-to-day

A simple way to picture it:

- **Daytime:** your panels power the home directly, and any surplus is exported and credited.
- **Evening and night:** with the sun down, you draw from the grid, drawing down your credits.
- **Billing period:** TANGEDCO nets your import against your export, so your bill reflects only the difference.

A special **bi-directional (net) meter** records both directions, which is why a meter change is part of the process.

## The TANGEDCO net-metering process and paperwork

Broadly, connecting a solar system for net metering involves:

1. **Application** to TANGEDCO for net metering, with your system details.
2. **Technical feasibility / approval** from the discom.
3. **Installation** of your solar system by your vendor.
4. **Net meter installation** and connection by TANGEDCO.
5. **Commissioning** — your system goes live and export credits begin.

The exact forms, fees and timelines are set by TANGEDCO and can be revised, so always work from the current official procedure.

## On-grid vs hybrid: what happens during power cuts

An important safety point: standard **on-grid** systems switch off during a grid outage, so a plain net-metered system won't power your home during a power cut. If backup during cuts matters to you, a **hybrid** system with batteries keeps essential loads running while still exporting surplus when the grid is up. ## What to expect on your bill after solar

Once net metering is live, your monthly TANGEDCO bill should drop substantially — for many well-sized home systems, to a small fraction of what it was. Exactly how far depends on your consumption pattern and system size. The key change is that you're paying for *net* grid usage, not everything you consume.
